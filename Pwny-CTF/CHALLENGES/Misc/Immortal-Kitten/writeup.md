## Resolution method
1. **The idea**

Windows console game “NINE LIVES: A Cat's Tale”: survive 10 situations with 9 lives, but **all options lead to death** → impossible to win normally. The hint (“dereference the address of the life variable”) suggests a memory patch (Cheat Engine style). But the flag displayed at victory is **generated by an XOR routine** which is decrypted **statically**, without playing or patching.

1. `strings`: no clear flag, but `Here's your flag: ` and `Congratulations, you magnificent hacker cat!` → the flag is produced on victory.
2. **Disassemble** (`objdump -d -M intel`) and locate a decryption loop: `xor r13b, BYTE PTR [rbp+r15-0x28]` with `and eax,0x7` → XOR by repeated key (8 bytes).
3. **Extract key + ciphertext** (function @0x14000abc0):
   - 8 byte key: dwords `0x38b4ff0a`, `0x89721348` → `0a ff b4 38 48 13 72 89`;
   - ciphertext: 9 dwords written to `[rbp-0x28]` (36 bytes);
   - `flag[i] = ciphertext[i] XOR key[i & 7]`.
4. Reproduce in Python → readable flag (36 bytes).
